**Subject:** What K-pop fans actually know — 17,425 scored quiz attempts

Hi Jeff,

Your write-up of Billboard's fandom report pulled out the stats that surprised you, so this
might land: we run a K-pop quiz site, and we've published what our players actually got
right and wrong. Not a survey — 17,425 scored attempts, May to August.

The pattern is a cliff. Fans are at 97.5% naming BTS members and 41.6% on BTS eras. Same
group, same population, weeks apart, 56 points apart. The five hardest quizzes on the site
are all catalogue and history questions about the biggest groups; the five easiest are all
"who is in this group".

The part that might interest you more: we set out to publish a ranking of fandoms by
knowledge and killed it. It looked real until we adjusted for the fact that big fandoms get
harder quizzes, and then it flipped sign depending on which months we used. That's in the
report, with the numbers.

## 2. Glenn Peoples / Billboard Pro

*Architect of the fandom report. We are not competing with him — we are the measurement his
survey cannot make. Lead with the complement, not the contrast.*

**Subject:** A complement to the K-Pop Fandom report — measured knowledge, 17,425 attempts

Hi Glenn,

Your K-Pop Fandom in the U.S. report asked 1,400 fans what they buy and stream. We've just
published the other half of that picture from a different instrument: we run a K-pop quiz
site and scored 17,425 attempts between May and August, so we can measure what fans know
rather than what they report.

The gap between the two is the interesting bit. Identity questions run above 95%. Catalogue
questions — albums, B-sides, debut years — fall to the low 40s. The most engaged fandoms are
not the highest scoring, and when we tried to build a ranking out of that, it did not
survive our own controls, which we've published rather than buried.

Method, sample sizes, queries and the snapshot boundary are all public beside the report. If
any of it is useful alongside your own data, the raw table is yours.

## 3. Music Ally

*Industry-side and data-literate; covered the Global K-Chart adding fan-activity data. They
care about how fan engagement gets measured at all.*

**Subject:** A new way to measure fandom depth, and the four findings it rejected

Hello,

You covered the Global K-Chart folding fan activity into its methodology, which is why this
might be relevant: we've published a report built on a measurement nobody in this space runs
— quiz scores. 17,425 scored attempts across 76 quizzes, May to August, on our K-pop quiz
site.

The findings are secondary to the method, honestly. What we think is worth a look is that we
tested four results to destruction and published the three that died: a fandom knowledge
ranking that reversed depending on the window, a girl-group / boy-group gap that vanished
under format matching, and a generation gradient resting on four groups. The one that
survived is that fans know people and not catalogues.

Everything is beside the report — queries, denominators, the discarded findings and the
exact snapshot boundary.

## 4. The Korea Herald, K-pop desk

*English-language Korean outlet with a standing K-pop section. Lead with the concrete and
translatable: the hardest and easiest quizzes.*

**Subject:** The hardest K-pop quiz questions, measured on 17,425 attempts

Hello,

We run a K-pop quiz site and have published what fans actually score, from 17,425 attempts
between May and August this year.

The concrete version: the hardest quiz on the site is BLACKPINK world records at 40%, then a
Stray Kids song-guessing quiz at 41.5%, then a BTS eras quiz at 41.6% on 781 attempts. The
easiest are all about members and fandom names, above 96%. More than one attempt in three is
a perfect score.

The through-line is that fans have the rosters memorised and the discographies not at all,
and that the biggest groups are the hardest to be a fan of by this measure — because a deep
catalogue is what makes a hard question possible.

Full figures and queries are published beside the report if any of it is useful.
