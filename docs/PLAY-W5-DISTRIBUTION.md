# W5 DISTRIBUTION — who gets the K-pop Knowledge Report, and what each one is told

My own plan says the distribution list is decided **before** the writing, and we did not do
that. This is the catch-up. A data report nobody pitches earns zero links, and links are the
only reason W5 exists — Domain Rating 1/100 is the constraint, not traffic.

**Verification status.** Every outlet below was found by searching, not from memory, and the
sources are listed at the end. What I could NOT verify and will not invent: current editor
email addresses, and the exact current self-promotion rules of any subreddit. Both must be
read at send time. I would rather this document have a hole in it than a plausible-looking
address that bounces or gets us banned.

---

## The positioning, and it is the whole pitch

Billboard already publishes a "K-Pop Fandom in the U.S." report. It **surveyed over 1,400
fans** about what they buy, stream and spend. Luminate does the same for consumption. So the
"K-pop fan data" beat is occupied — but every incumbent asks fans what they *think* and what
they *do with money*.

Nobody measures what fans **know**, because nobody else has the instrument. We do: 17,425
scored attempts.

> **Billboard asked 1,400 fans what they think. We scored 17,425 attempts of what they know.**

That is the sentence. It is true, it is checkable, and it positions us as complementary to
the incumbent rather than competing with it — which is also why the incumbent's own
audience is reachable.

The second, stranger asset is the report's honesty section: we tested four findings to
destruction and published the four that died. That is not a K-pop story, it is a *data*
story, and it reaches a different audience that links far more readily than entertainment
media does.

## TIER 1 — the ones worth a personal, specific pitch

These cover data about K-pop and **link to primary sources**. Four or five real pitches here
are worth more than a hundred blind sends.

**Jeff Benjamin — The Crossover (the-xover.com).** The strongest match on this list. He
covers the K-pop/global crossover, and his treatment of the Billboard report was literally
"11 Most Surprising Stats", linking straight to the original and crediting its author by
name. His format *is* our deliverable.
→ Pitch him **the format finding**: fans score 90%+ on who's in the group and fall to 40% on
what the group released. Lead with the five hardest quizzes; they are all deep-catalogue
questions about the biggest groups.

**Billboard Pro / Glenn Peoples.** Architect of the fandom report. We are not competing with
him, we are the measurement his survey cannot make.
→ Pitch the **complement**: his report says what fans spend, ours says what they know, and
the gap between fandom confidence and fandom accuracy is the story neither dataset tells
alone.

**Music Ally (musically.com).** Industry-side, data-literate, covered the Global K-Chart
adding fan-activity data. They care about how fan engagement gets measured at all.
→ Pitch the **method**: a new instrument for measuring fandom depth, and the four findings
it was honest enough to reject.

**The Korea Herald, K-pop desk.** English-language Korean outlet with a standing K-pop
section, reaches the domestic industry.
→ Pitch the **hardest-quizzes list**, which is concrete, translatable and fandom-specific.

## TIER 2 — big reach, weak links, still worth sending

Soompi, allkpop, Koreaboo. Large audiences, and their forums (allkpop, kpopsource) already
picked up the Billboard report — that is where fans find these things. Expect nofollow links
or none at all.
→ Send it, but book it as **discovery**, not as authority. Its value is that a Tier 1
journalist sees a fandom already arguing about our numbers.
→ Pitch: the easiest and hardest quizzes, and the "one in five attempts is perfect" line.

## TIER 3 — statistics aggregators, and a warning

Sites like wifitalents and gitnux publish "K-pop statistics 2026" roundups and cite whatever
they can find, with links. They will take our numbers.

**They are also low-quality aggregators and their links carry little weight.** Submitting
costs nothing, so do it, but a strategy built on them is a strategy built on nothing, and at
DR 1 we cannot afford to look like a site that only low-quality sites cite. **Four Tier 1
placements beat forty of these.** Recorded here so nobody later mistakes volume for progress.

## TIER 4 — communities, where the rules matter more than the pitch

**r/kpop.** Large and strict. Its self-promotion rules must be read in full before anything
is posted — I could not retrieve them reliably and I am not going to guess. If original data
is permitted at all, it is permitted as a text post with the findings *in* the post, not as a
link.

**r/dataisbeautiful.** Wants a visualisation and an [OC] tag with the source stated. This is
where the honesty angle lands best: a chart of the four findings we discarded is a better
r/dataisbeautiful post than any of our surviving numbers.

**Standing rule, from the W6 doctrine and it applies here:** we post as ourselves, we never
plant, we never sockpuppet. Given what we found in our own March–April data, a site
publishing a report about measurement integrity cannot afford to fake its own distribution.

## The pitch itself

One per outlet, never a blast. The template that fails is "here is our report". The one that
works names the thing that outlet already cares about, then offers the measurement:

> Your piece on Billboard's fandom report pulled out the stats that surprised you. We have a
> dataset that answers a question that report could not: not what fans spend, but what they
> actually know. 17,425 scored quiz attempts, May to August. Fans are at 90%+ on member
> questions and 40% on discography. The hardest quiz on our site is a BLACKPINK one, at 40%.
> Full figures, queries and sample sizes are public, including the four findings we tested
> and threw away.

Attach nothing. Link to the report and to the data file. Offer the raw numbers on request —
the offer is what separates us from a brand doing PR.

## Sequence

1. Owner reviews DRAFT v2 and approves the findings.
2. The report gets a landing page with the full dataset published beside it. Nothing is
   pitched before the page exists — a pitch that lands on nothing is spent.
3. Tier 1, individually, four or five emails, each one naming that outlet's own recent work.
4. Tier 2 and Tier 3 a week later, once there is something to point at.
5. Tier 4 last, after reading each community's rules.
