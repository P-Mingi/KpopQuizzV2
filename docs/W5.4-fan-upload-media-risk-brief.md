# W5.4 - Fan-upload media: risk brief and decision request

Status: DECISION REQUIRED (owner). This is a brief, not a build. Nothing in W5.4 is implemented.
Scope: whether Verse should let fans upload their own images (photocard scans, merch/lightstick
photos, fancams, event photos, avatars beyond presets) rather than the current curator-entered,
approved-host-only model.

## 1. Where we are today (the baseline this would change)

Verse currently ships **zero user-uploaded media**. Every image is either:

- an official/first-party asset we control (`/public`, our Supabase bucket), or
- a **hotlinked** URL that a curator pastes, gated to a small allowlist in
  `apps/quiz/src/lib/image-hosts.ts`: `i.pinimg.com`, `lh3.googleusercontent.com`,
  `drive.google.com`, `cdn.discordapp.com`, `i.ytimg.com`, and `*.supabase.co`.

Photocards (136) and collectibles (138) both keep `image_url` null until a curator supplies a
legal source. There is no upload endpoint, no user file storage, no image moderation pipeline.

Two things worth naming honestly about the baseline:

1. The allowlist is a **crash-safety** guard (next/image throws on un-configured hosts), not a
   licensing guarantee. Several allowed hosts (Pinterest, Discord CDN, Google Drive) are
   themselves user-generated-content hosts. Hotlinking a Pinterest image is still someone else's
   copyright and can rot or be pulled. So even today the "strict-legal" claim rests on curator
   discipline, not on a technical license check.
2. Because a human curator is in the loop for every image, the volume is low and the blast radius
   of a bad image is one row a curator can fix.

Fan uploads remove that human-in-the-loop and invert the volume assumption. That is the whole risk.

## 2. What "fan uploads" would actually introduce

Letting users upload images turns Verse from a publisher of vetted content into a **host of
third-party content**. That is a different legal and operational category. The risks, roughly in
order of severity:

### Legal / liability
- **Copyright + DMCA.** Photocard scans, concert photos and merch shots are overwhelmingly owned
  by the agency, the photographer, or the fan-site ("fansite masters" are aggressive about
  their photos). Hosting them ourselves (not hotlinking) makes us the distributor. DMCA safe
  harbor is available but **conditional**: we must register a DMCA agent, publish a notice-and-
  takedown policy, act on takedowns expeditiously, and terminate repeat infringers. None of that
  exists yet.
- **Illegal content (hard stop).** Any open image upload can receive CSAM. US law requires
  detection/reporting (NCMEC) and there is no "we're small" exemption. This alone means an upload
  path cannot ship without hash-matching (e.g. PhotoDNA-class) and a reporting process.
- **Likeness / privacy of living persons.** Verse's standing stance is "no personal data on living
  persons." Fan photos of idols (and of other fans at events) reintroduce personal likeness and
  potentially minors. NCII (non-consensual intimate imagery) and doxxing via EXIF geodata are
  live risks on any fan-photo feed.
- **Trademark / brand.** Lightstick and merch shots carry group/agency trademarks; user captions
  can create false endorsement or counterfeit-marketplace impressions.
- **Defamation / harassment vectors** via captions and image content.

### Operational / cost
- **Storage + bandwidth + CDN** for real image volume (previously $0 for media).
- **Moderation is the real cost.** Automated filters cannot clear copyright or likeness questions;
  those need human review. A submission queue at fandom scale is an ongoing staffing commitment,
  not a one-time build.
- **Reputation/brand safety.** One offensive or infringing image on a group's space is a
  screenshot; the reputational cost is asymmetric to the feature's upside.

### Product
- Upside is real but narrow: user photocard scans make the collection checklist feel alive, and
  merch/lightstick galleries look better with real photos than placeholders. The question is
  whether that upside justifies standing up host-of-UGC obligations.

## 3. Options

**Option A - Stay curator-only (status quo, recommended for now).**
No uploads. Curators keep entering approved-host images with a source. Zero new legal surface.
Cost: galleries stay sparse until curators do the work; no "my own scan" delight.

**Option B - Fan submissions into a curator review queue (the only defensible way to add fans).**
Fans *submit* an image (URL or upload); it lands **unpublished** in a moderation queue; a curator
must approve before it is ever public. Keeps a human in the loop and keeps us a publisher, not an
open host. Still requires: DMCA agent + policy, CSAM hash-scan on any true file upload (URL-only
submission avoids storage but not copyright), EXIF stripping, a takedown workflow, and the queue
UI + curator staffing. This is a **medium-to-large** build plus a permanent moderation cost, and
needs legal sign-off, not just engineering.

**Option C - Open fan uploads with post-hoc moderation.**
Publish-then-review. Not recommended: it maximizes legal exposure (we host infringing/illegal
content live before anyone looks) for the least defensible benefit. List it only to reject it.

## 4. Recommendation

Hold at **Option A** for launch. If fan media becomes a priority, do **Option B** and treat it as
its own workstream with three gates before any code:

1. **Legal gate** - register a DMCA agent, write the AUP/ToS upload clause + notice-and-takedown
   policy, decide the CSAM-scanning provider. (Owner + counsel; I cannot do this.)
2. **Policy gate** - a written content policy: what's allowed (own photos of own items),
   what's banned (fansite photos without permission, images of people/minors, anything off the
   item itself), EXIF/geodata stripping, repeat-infringer termination.
3. **Design gate** - submission -> unpublished -> curator-approve flow, storage vs URL-only
   decision, moderator tooling, reporting button.

Only after those three would W5.4 become a buildable step. Until then it stays parked.

## 5. What I am NOT doing

Per instruction, W5.4 is not built. No upload endpoint, no storage bucket wiring, no schema, no UI.
This document is the deliverable; the decision is yours.
